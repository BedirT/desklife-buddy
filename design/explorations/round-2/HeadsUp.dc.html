Level 1 · The approach

A sunset in the notch.

Forty-five seconds before a break is due, a small capsule slides out from under the notch — always the same place, top-center. Inside it is a tiny sky, and the sun in it carries two things at once. Its height is the time: it starts high and sinks for the whole 45 seconds. Its place is you: it drifts along the tiny sky to keep station above wherever your pointer is on the screen — it doesn't chase you, it accompanies you, and it moves only when you move. When it touches its little horizon, the break opens, and the full-screen sky continues the same sunset at full size.

Holds come only from things the app can honestly know: your manual hold, working hours, optional Calendar busy time, a Focus filter, or apps you named yourself. It never watches other apps to guess. On displays without a notch the capsule docks under the menu-bar clock; if you prefer notifications, that stays first-class.
The character, in five rules
DoesSink once, slowly, for the whole heads-up — a single eased motion that carries the time. And keep station over you: its place along the tiny sky mirrors your pointer's place on the screen, so a glance up always finds it right above where you're working. It moves only when you move — never on its own.
DoesKeep its two closed eyes — resting is the whole character. The app's own posture is calm company, someone dozing in the corner of the room while you work, and that reads as care.
DoesHave small one-shot moments: a soft settle when it lands on the horizon, a slow, contented eye-crinkle when a break runs its course — the capsule returns for about two seconds at the end to play it, then leaves. Each plays once, eased, with a static Reduce Motion equivalent. Premium is precise, not busy.
NeverWears the face on the break surfaces — there the instruction is to look away, and a face invites eye contact. On the break horizon the sun is the plain render.
NeverLoops, pulses, or gets louder as time runs out. And a plain text-only heads-up remains a first-class setting, not a fallback.
