Cadence · Round 2

A break is a place,
not a dialog.

Round 1 mistook calm for joyless — three shades of grey type on a dim field. This one keeps every honesty rule and throws out the austerity. Each break surface is a landscape: the instruction sits in the sky, your controls sit on the ground, and the horizon line is the progress. Nothing pulses, nothing loops, nothing counts down at you.

Visual reset — far
Deep dusk, a low distant sun, vast sky. The field itself depicts the instruction: look at something a long way off. 20 seconds, about every 25 minutes.
Movement — near
The same landscape an hour later. Closer, warmer, more saturated. You know which break it is from the colour temperature alone, before you read a word.
Light appearance
The same composition at a different hour, not an inversion. This is the answer to the round-1 question of whether the break screen should track the system appearance: yes, by changing key.

Taken from LookAway

  • Warmth and saturation. A break screen should be a pleasant place to arrive, and theirs is. Ours is warmer still and, unlike theirs, says which clock it belongs to.
  • Colourful glyph tiles in Settings — which is not their invention, it is what System Settings itself does. Native, and it makes a preferences list enjoyable to scroll.
  • One italic serif word per headline. Apple ships New York as a system serif, so this costs nothing and reads unmistakably Mac.
  • Glass pill controls floating over the field, with their shortcut printed on them.

Deliberately not taken

  • Their two headline features are posture nudges and blink reminders — camera-adjacent sensing our research puts out of scope entirely. Their differentiation is perception; ours is honesty.
  • Their strictest enforcement tier locks the Mac and forbids skipping. Cadence cannot ever do that — the menu bar stays lit above every break surface as proof.
  • One gradient for every break. We have two clocks, so we get two atmospheres — the thing they can't do.
  • Their eye character that follows the cursor. The mechanic is honest — the pointer is the only thing either app can perceive — but the eye is their signature, and on this product it would whisper gaze sensing. Ours is the sun: it descends to the horizon through the heads-up window and leans its glint toward your pointer while it waits. See the Heads-up artboard.
